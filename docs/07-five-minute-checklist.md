# The Five-Minute AI Terms Checklist (Bookmark This)

This is the whole guide squeezed into one page. Bookmark it, print it, or keep
it in a note on your phone. Any time you sign up for a new AI app - or one you
already use posts "we've updated our terms" - run through this list for five
minutes and you will know more than 95% of users.

## How to use this checklist

1. Open the app's terms of service (sometimes called Terms of Use, Terms of
   Service, or Conditions of Use) and its privacy policy. Both matter; the
   training clause often lives in one and the retention window in the other.
2. Use your browser's search (`Ctrl+F` / `Cmd+F`) or the app's help center to
   find each keyword below. Five minutes is enough.
3. Square brackets, like `[this]`, mean "fill in what you found."

## The checklist

### 1. Training on your chats

- Search: *"improve," "train," "training," "machine learning."*
- Find: whether they may use your conversations to train or improve models.
- Settings: check for a toggle ("Data controls," "Help improve the model,"
  "Improve the model for everyone") and record its default:
  [ on / off / not set ]
- Is the default **on** for the plan you are signing up for?
- Is there a business/education tier that promises no training on your data?
- Is there a temporary/incognito mode?

### 2. Data retention

- Search: *"retention," "delete," "backup," "archive," "as long as."*
- Find: how long chats survive after you delete them, and any "we keep this
  indefinitely" language for de-identified or aggregated data.
- Where does "delete" land your chats: [ visible / soft-deleted /
  fully deleted ]?
- Is their **auto-delete** option offered? (24 hours / 7 / 30 / 90 days /
  18 months - whatever is available.)
- Any carve-out for data kept longer due to **legal requests** or **safety
  investigations**? (This is normal; just note that it exists.)

### 3. Age limits

- Search: *"age," "years old," "minimum age," "children."*
- Find: the minimum age to use this app, and whether children under it may
  use it with a parent's account.
- Minimum age: [ 13 / 16 / 18 / other ].
- Does your state or country require higher, and is that referenced?
- Is there an education or youth version, if a minor needs to use it?

### 4. Export rights

- Search: *"export," "download your data," "copy of your data," "portability."*
- Find: what the app lets you take with you and how you get it.
- Is there a one-click export? What formats? [ json / zip / other ]
- Can you export **before** closing the account (yes is the good answer)?
- Do you **own** your prompts and the outputs you create? (Find the sentence
  that says so. If you can't find it, that itself is worth noting.)

### 5. Account suspension

- Search: *"suspend," "terminate," "discretion," "no reason," "notice."*
- Find: what the company can stop serving you for, and what (if anything)
  you get to appeal it with.
- Do they need a **reason** to suspend you? Write down the wording.
- Is there an **appeal** or a support path you can find right now, before you
  ever need it?
- Arbitration: is there a **dispute resolution** clause, and importantly, an
  **opt-out window** and deadline?
  [ opt-out allowed within ____ days ]

### 6. Change-of-terms notice

- Search: *"may update," "changes to," "notice," "continued use."*
- Find: how, and how far in advance, they tell you the terms changed.
- How much warning do you get: [ none / in-app notice / email / 30 days ],
- and does continuing to use the app count as accepting the new terms?

## Tiebreaker: is this app right at all?

Two questions to answer before signing up, which no terms page will answer
for you:

- **Do I actually need this?** A new AI signup costs nothing today and a
  little of your attention forever. Five minutes of this checklist is the
  *minimum* honest price.
- **What's my exit plan if I leave?** Where will your prompts and documents
  live when this app is no longer the one in your day?

## Quick reality check

> **Friend note:** The first time you do this it takes eight-ish minutes and a
> little grumbling; the third time it takes four, and the tenth time you flick
> through an entire terms document in the time it takes your coffee to brew.
> The goal was never "read everything." The goal was "know the six things that
> actually bite people." You now do.
