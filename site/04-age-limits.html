Age Limits: The Rule Almost Every Family Breaks

Almost every consumer AI app's terms of service includes an age requirement. It is usually one short sentence that nobody reads, and it is the one clause that gets violated the most - usually by accident, by loving parents.

What the clause actually says

It almost always reads something like this (wording varies, meaning does not):

"You must be at least 13 years old, or the minimum age in your jurisdiction, to use this service. If your child uses the service, you are responsible."

Or, for stricter apps:

"You must be 18 years or older to use this service."

A few patterns worth knowing:

  • 13 is the common floor in the United States, because federal children's privacy law (COPPA) uses 13 as the line where apps can legally collect data from kids on their own.
  • Some countries set stricter floors. A handful of countries require parental consent (not just parental permission) up to 14, 15, or 16. Good term sheets say "13 or your jurisdiction's minimum, whichever is higher."
  • Some AI tools simply require 18. Certain image generators and "adult capabilities" tools, and some AI companions/roleplay apps, intentionally keep the bar at 18.
  • Schools and workplaces often have their own rules on top of the app's rule. A teacher letting a whole class use a 13+ chatbot is not automatically safe; the school or the district may have a stricter policy (and COPPA obligations if the kids are younger).

What the fine print actually means

When the terms say "you are responsible for children who use the service," the practical translations are:

  • If you let a 10-year-old use your account, you are the one breaking the terms, not the kid.
  • If anything goes sideways - a suspicous charge, a suspension, a scary conversation - your leverage with the company's support team is significantly worse, because you were the one in the wrong.
  • If you share your account with a younger child, you are usually also violating the "one account per person" clause that most consumer apps have. Two violations are usually stored together in the terms.

Just to be clear: this chapter is not a lecture about bad parenting. Kids are curious. AI is the new toy. This chapter exists so that when your kid asks "can I use ChatGPT for homework?" you can answer with actual information instead of a startled expression.

Practical rules for families

If a minor in your house wants to use AI tools, here is a workable setup:

  1. Check the app's minimum age first. It is on the very first screen you scroll past during sign-up, or searchable in the app's own help pages.
  2. Check for a dedicated youth or education version. Some major AI apps now offer student or education modes with content guardrails. These are a genuinely different product; prefer them when available.
  3. Create supervision, not a secret. Let the kid use AI in a shared space on a family device, rather than on their own phone in their bedroom. Not because AI is scary, but because the same supervision logic you would apply to any other account applies here too.
  4. Set up a shared account email you control. If the account belongs to you (with the child as a supervised user, where offered), you keep the password and see the prompts.
  5. Teach the "don't tell the robot everything" rule. Kids (and adults!) should know that whatever they type can be stored and, depending on the settings, used for training - exactly as covered in chapter 2.

One more thing: age limits on features, not just accounts

Some apps restrict specific features by age. Common examples:

  • Conversational memory and personalization may be disabled for younger users.
  • Voice chats or image generation may be locked behind age thresholds.
  • Sharing and publishing content can have their own age gates.

So even when the account is fine, the feature might not be. That is okay - it is not a trap; it is the app trying not to run into the same problem that every social network has had to work around.

Quick reality check

Friend note: I am not trying to scare parents. Consumer AI apps are generally not more dangerous to a supervised teen than the rest of the internet. The point of this chapter is narrower: the age clause is the one legal sentence in the terms that a normal family is most likely to actually violate, and building a small, boring routine around it turns a legally tricky situation into a normal one.
